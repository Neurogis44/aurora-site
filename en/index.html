The big review

A big review of all the code added since 1.0.9, before showing Aurora to the world: over 70 fixes. Your pump, your files and your privacy are better protected, and Windows is watched more closely.

WINDOWS

Your Windows version, kept in check

Aurora tells you when your version of Windows is no longer supported by Microsoft, or won't be in less than two months: without security updates, a PC stays exposed.

  • Windows 11 24H2, Home and Pro editions, stops being supported on October 13, 2026: Aurora already says so.
  • No more “up to date” when Windows Update is off, or hasn't checked for days.
  • Updates for Office and Microsoft's other products are no longer taken for Windows ones.
ALSO

Your pump, your files and your privacy

If Aurora doesn't know where your pump is plugged in, its modes don't touch any fan: it first asks you to pick it. And if FanControl, Argus Monitor or SpeedFan already set your fans, it lets them do their job.

  • Duplicate files finally look in your OneDrive folders, never in the PC's other accounts or in programs, and never send more than your Recycle Bin can keep.
  • The report for your technician: your games and programs, if you hide them, are no longer named anywhere, and its preview is deleted when Aurora closes.
  • A new version of Aurora: “Later” hides the banner for a day, then it comes back until it's installed.
  • Terminal asks less of your graphics card and processor, and a skin that can no longer be read tells you why.
WHAT IT DOES

It no longer just measures: it understands.

Its tabs turn numbers into answers: is my PC doing well, what changed, why is my game struggling — and which Windows update to install.

HEALTH

Is your PC OK? Aurora tells you.

A check-up redone every second that only talks about what is wrong, most serious first — and also tells you when all is well.

  • Your build: a display plugged into the motherboard or set too low, a graphics card in the wrong slot.
  • Drives: SMART wear, running out of space, heat.
  • A fan or the pump giving up, a BIOS battery getting weak.
  • A power supply starting to sag: its 12 V tracked under load, if your motherboard measures it.
  • What Windows logs without a word: hardware errors, blue screens, sudden shutdowns.
  • Heat creeping up over the months: dust shows before it costs you.
  • A full report for whoever helps you remotely, ready to send.
  • A notification — and an alarm if you want — when it is urgent.

“It taught me that my graphics card draws 50 W while doing nothing: my three displays keep it from slowing down.” — Denis

CHANGES

The time machine

Your PC has been slow since Tuesday? Aurora takes a snapshot of it every ten minutes and notes what changes: software, drivers, services, startup programs, Windows updates, BIOS.

  • In the Health tab, a problem comes with what changed just before it.
  • The effect of every change, measured before and after: your games, your temperatures, your startup.
  • Windows startup timed — to the desktop, ready, shutdown — and who slows it down.
  • The noise filtered out: drivers that come and go, Defender updates.
GAMES

Your games, over time

Every session is kept: average FPS, 1 % low, duration. Aurora notices when a game runs slower than it used to.

  • An overlay on top of your games, in its own small window, without touching the game.

NO SURPRISES

What Aurora does not do

Privacy

No account, no ads, no readings sent, nobody tracked. It asks the Internet three things, and only three: once a day, the latest version number; the speed test, when you click it; and your message, when you write to support from Settings — with your hardware summary only if you tick the box (models, versions, sensors; never your name, address, games or software). The first can be turned off in Settings; the other two never go out on their own.

Signed, written in Rust

Aurora and its installer are digitally signed in the name of Denis Desbiens: Windows knows where the file comes from, and would warn you if anyone altered it along the way. And Aurora is written in Rust, a modern language built for safety: it prevents memory errors, the cause of most security holes. That matters for a program that runs as administrator.

What you need

Windows 10 (1809 or newer) or 11, 64-bit. Administrator rights, asked for at startup: that is the only way to read temperature sensors. The installer adds .NET 10 and the PawnIO driver if needed.

INSTALLATION

Step by step, no surprises

A new version has no reputation with Microsoft yet: for the first few days, Edge may be wary of it, even though it's signed. Here's what to do, with the real windows.

  1. Download the file

    If Edge says it “isn't commonly downloaded”, that just means it's still new.

  2. Keep it

    Hover over the file, click the three dots “…”, then “Keep”.

  3. Check the publisher

    Edge asks you to confirm. The publisher should be Denis Desbiens: click the arrow next to “Delete”, then “Keep anyway”.

  4. Open it

    Windows asks for administrator rights. The window shows “Verified publisher: Denis Desbiens”: click “Yes”.

  5. Let it work

    The installation adds .NET 10 and PawnIO if they are missing: that can take a few minutes, bar frozen. This is normal. Aurora opens when you click “Finish”.

  • More header names recognized on ASUS, ASRock and Gigabyte boards; VRM and chipset fans stay with the motherboard.

SINCE 1.0.7

Measured, not promised

What limits your FPS, live. The effect of every change on your PC. And a tab to tune Windows, where everything can be put back.

IN GAME

What limits your FPS

During a game, the overlay tells you in one word what's holding your frame rate back. Not a guess from a parts list: a measurement, on your PC, while you play.

  • “GPU”: your graphics card is maxed out. That's the normal situation.
  • “CPU”: one processor core is saturated and holding it back — even when the processor shows under 10% overall.
  • “FPS CAP”: VSync or a frame limiter. Nothing to fix, and the processor isn't wrongly blamed.
  • Over 30 days, “What to upgrade first?” (Games tab): the card, the processor, the memory… or nothing at all.

“My Radeon RX 9070 XT sets the pace 99.8% of the gaming time, and my Ryzen 9 9900X almost never holds it back. Aurora's verdict: no rush.” — Denis

CHANGES

The effect of every change

A new graphics driver, a BIOS, a Windows update, a startup program: Aurora measures on its own what it did, before and after.

  • Your games (frames per second), your temperatures at equal load, your startup.
  • Never a verdict without something to compare: three sessions, three startups, an hour of load on each side.
  • What you do yourself can be noted too: dust, thermal paste, graphics card pads.
  • A notification when the verdict comes in.

“Aurora measured what Spotify, launched at startup, adds to my startup: 1.8 seconds.” — Denis

NEW TAB

Tune Windows, and put it all back

The Windows tab reads everything — startup, gaming settings, displays, memory, privacy, small annoyances — and only changes what you check, through Windows' official means.

  • Never the protections: Defender, firewall, User Account Control, updates.
  • The original value written down before any change: “Restore” in one click, “Restore everything”, and uninstalling puts it all back.
  • Measured rather than promised: the time saved at startup, and a display's refresh rate tried for 15 seconds before it's kept.
  • Maintenance: check and repair Windows (DISM, SFC), check your drives, old temporary files to the Recycle Bin.

Ryzen X3D chips

On a 7900X3D, 7950X3D, 9900X3D or 9950X3D, automatic power management keeps the mode AMD asks for while you play, the Windows tab checks the rest (Game Mode, Game Bar, driver), and shows which CCD the game runs on.

The real restart

Windows fast startup doesn't really restart: Aurora shows it in grey in the Changes tab, and tells you how long Windows hasn't really restarted.

An overlay that fits

Each theme has its own measurements, taken with the longest values: in the Forge theme, the bar's “Expand” button is no longer cut off.

WEEK AFTER WEEK

Your PC compared with itself

In the Health tab, one bar per week. Each week is compared with your first ones, at the same workload and allowing for the air in your case: if the bars rise, it's your PC changing, not the summer.

  • How warm the graphics card, its hotspot and the processor run: dust, thermal paste drying out.

  • How long Windows takes to start, until the PC is ready.

  • The 12 V, at rest and under load, if your motherboard measures it.
